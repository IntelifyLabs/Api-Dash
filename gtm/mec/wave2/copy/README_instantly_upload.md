## The angle, corrected

Showhouse is a white-label AI visualiser **and lead engine**. A shopper
photographs their own room, the brand's product appears in it, a guided finder
turns their answers into a written brief, and the image unlocks only once they
verify an email. The brand receives render, brief and a real address as one
record.

The two earlier framings were each half right and wrong on their own. The first
sold "a design tool", which is a feature Daltile and Marazzi give away free.
The second sold the lead record, which is true but abstract in line one. The
site resolves it in a sentence: **a visualiser is the hook, the pipeline is the
product.**

So the copy **opens on the mechanic**, because your tile on their own floor is
instantly picturable, and **closes on the pipeline**, because that is what pays
for it.

## What the page fixed that no rewrite would have

1. **The product is called Showhouse.** Nothing in v1 or v2 said so. Now named
   in message 1 on all 390 rows.
2. **`/stone` and `/bathroom` do not exist.** Both were guesses and both were
   404s. Real studios: `/tile`, `/rugs`, `/wallpaper`, `/countertops`,
   `/mosaic`. Stone routes to `/tile`, which is where the site puts it: "tile
   showrooms, stone yards, surface distributors".
3. **There is no self-serve trial**, so "start a trial" was an offer we could
   not honour. The real ladder is: open a live studio, which needs no sign in,
   then a demo where the team arrives with the prospect's catalogue loaded.
4. **The studios render a visitor's own photo.** That makes message 1's ask
   genuinely free: open a page, upload a photo.

Wording is borrowed from the page where it is sharper than mine: *before they
order a sample*, *every wall becomes a live product page*, *without touching
anything else in the photo*, *live in weeks, not quarters*.

## Message 1 is an A/B, both arms in this one file

Every row carries **two complete message 1s**. Instantly picks one per
contact, so the test runs off a single upload with no second campaign and no
split list.

| Arm | Columns | What it argues |
|---|---|---|
| **R, revenue** | `msg_subject_1a`, `msg_body_1a` | The visitor you never meet. They look, they cannot picture it in their own room, they leave with no name attached. |
| **C, cost** | `msg_subject_1b`, `msg_body_1b` | The sample you post to someone who was never going to order. Put the decision in front of the sample. |

`ab_arm` records which arm the row was dealt. The split is **by company**, so
both people at a two-person company are always on the same arm and never get
two different pitches: 154 revenue, 152 cost, zero companies split. It is
dealt **within each segment** rather than by a coin flip, because a flip left
segment C at 33 against 41 and the result would not have been readable.

Subject and body are paired, not mixed. A cost subject over a revenue body
would move two variables at once and neither number would mean anything.

- **`msg_subject_1a` (revenue)** leads on what the catalogue cannot do:
  *Show your tiles in a buyer's own room* · *Your tiles, in their own room
  photo* · *Your catalogue can't show their room* · *Your collections, in their
  own room* · *Browsed your collections, then left* · *A PDF page, or their
  actual room*
- **`msg_subject_1b` (cost)** wraps the same product in the show each row came
  from: *Before Cersaie 2027, let them see it in the room* · *Your tiles in
  their rooms by Cersaie 2027* · *Stop posting samples before Cersaie 2027* ·
  *Fewer samples between now and Cersaie 2027*, and, for the 20 rows with no
  sourced show, *Send samples to buyers who already decided* · *Let them see it
  before you post a sample* · *Your tiles, seen before the sample ships* · *The
  samples that never become orders*.

**"tiles" is the row's own product**, taken from the studio it routes to, so a
mosaic manufacturer reads *mosaics* and a countertop manufacturer reads
*countertops*. That is the shortest way to make line one unmistakably theirs,
and unlike the domain it always fits inside 50 characters.

11 distinct subjects on the revenue arm and 22 on the cost arm. Largest single
share 19 and 16 per cent.

The show label comes from the row's own source directory. Cersaie 2026 ran 21
to 25 September 2026, so the forward reference is the 2027 edition: for a
manufacturer who just came off a stand that is the planning window, not a
deadline a year out.

Follow-ups are single copies, `msg_subject_2/3` and `msg_body_2/3`. Only
message 1 is under test.

## Bugs caught building v4

- **`hash()` randomises per process.** Python salts string hashing unless
  `PYTHONHASHSEED` is set, so the A/B arms and every rotated subject reshuffled
  on each rebuild. A contact on the revenue arm today could be on the cost arm
  tomorrow with nothing in the file showing it moved, which invalidates a
  running test in silence. Replaced with CRC32. Two builds are now identical.
- **Both people at a two-person company got the same message 1**, introduced by
  the arm rewrite. Thread B now opens on the outcome and closes by naming the
  colleague, so the two read as two people writing rather than one merge.
- **The 20 rows with no trade show had one cost subject between them**, because
  every show-bearing line dropped out and a single survivor was left. Four
  dateless lines added to that bank.
- **`Work Email` and `Work Email (2)` are two different people, and the columns
  are crossed.** They were read as two addresses for one person.
- **A second brand after a separator read as a merge field.** "GTILES -
  ROCKYLAND" and "Roca / Lamosa USA" wrote out in full mid-sentence, and the
  spaced hyphen was the only dash left in the file. Both now use the first
  segment, which is the brand the domain belongs to.
- **The `niche` column poisoned studio routing for the second time.** Every
  Cersaie row carries "Ceramic / Tile (verify)", which sent an interior-doors
  manufacturer to the tile studio. Dropped from routing entirely.

## Message 1 subjects: Set B, chosen 30 Sept

Three earlier sets were rejected for the same reason. Every one was written
from our chair: *Show your tiles in a buyer's own room*, *Retile their room,
not your catalogue*, *Every home becomes your showroom*. Each announces a
vendor in the first three words, and the brief was the opposite one — would
the person holding this inbox open it.

**Who that is.** A marketing manager, export director or owner at a tile or
surface manufacturer. **264 of 390 sit in Italy or Spain**, reading English as
a second language. 325 came off a Cersaie stand on 25 September, so this week
their inbox is post-show follow-ups, distributors chasing stock, and a wall of
vendors selling SEO, AI and stand space for 2027. They delete on sight
anything opening `Your <product>…`, anything starting with an imperative, and
anything that reads as a tagline.

So **no line contains our product, our benefit, or a verb we want them to
perform.** Each contains something of theirs: their domain, and the product
they actually sell.

| Frame | Rendered |
|---|---|
| `who runs {dom}?` | who runs 41zero42.com? |
| `who handles {dom}?` | who handles acquabella.com? |
| `who looks after {dom}?` | who looks after aipporte.com? |
| `a question about {dom}` | a question about apegrupo.com |
| `quick question about {dom}` | quick question about anatolia.com |
| `the {product} on {dom}` | the tiles on anatolia.com |
| `your {product} on {dom}` | your mosaics on gruppobardelli.com |
| `{product} on {dom}, one question` | tiles on alfa-lux.it, one question |
| `the {single} enquiries you never get` | the tile enquiries you never get |

**351 distinct subjects across 390 rows, largest share 6 per cent.** The
previous set ran 11 lines at 19 per cent, and near-identical phrasing across a
segment was copy defect four on campaign 2.

**Lower case on purpose.** The earlier rule here was that a lower-case subject
reads as a newsletter. That was backwards: newsletters arrive in Title Case,
and a lower-case line reads as something a person typed. These are questions,
not headlines.

**The subject is identical on both arms.** Message 1 is an A/B on the BODY,
revenue against cost. Varying the subject alongside it would move two things at
once and neither number would be readable.

## Job titles: the filter ships, the data does not exist yet

Asked for on 29 Sept, and worth being blunt about: **there are no job titles in
the source.** `contacts_final.csv` has 47 columns and not one of them is a
title. The waterfall returned names, emails and LinkedIn URLs only, so there is
nothing to filter on yet and nothing I can invent.

What is in the file now:

| Column | Today | Once titles land |
|---|---|---|
| `job_title` | `not enriched` on all 390 | the title as enriched |
| `qa_decision_maker` | `not enriched` on all 390 | `yes` / `no` / `review` |

And the path to fill it:

1. `python3 titles_worklist.py` writes **`titles_to_enrich.csv`**, 306 rows with
   the LinkedIn URL where one exists. **Coverage is 276 of 306.** The 30 without
   a profile are listed when the script runs.
2. Fill the `job_title` column however you want it enriched.
3. Save it as `titles.csv` beside the build script and rerun. Every row gets
   tagged and nothing else in the file changes.

The rule, once it runs: **`yes`** is whoever owns the website and the demand it
generates, so marketing, digital, ecommerce, sales and export leadership, plus
the owner or general manager at a company small enough to decide everything.
**`no`** is production, plant, quality, logistics, purchasing, administration
and technical staff. Real people at real target companies with no say over what
goes on the website. **`review`** is a title the rule has not seen, surfaced for
a person rather than filtered out quietly. "Production Manager" resolves to
`no`, because the exclusions are checked before the inclusions.

Tested against a throwaway titles file across all 306 rows: 184 `yes`, 92 `no`,
30 `review`, and "Production Manager", "Warehouse Supervisor" and "Quality
Control Technician" all landed on `no`.

**I have not run an enrichment for this.** Clay is the obvious route and the
standing instruction is not to touch it without being asked, so say the word
and it takes one pass.

## Deliverability: every message on every row, not a sample

Asked for on 29 Sept: run the templates through Mail Meteor's spam checker,
check all three emails across all variants.

Mail Meteor's checker is a browser tool with no API, and this container has no
outbound route to it, so **`spam_audit.py` runs the same classes of check
locally over all 1,224 emails** — 306 rows times four messages — rather than
over a handful pasted by hand. It checks graded trigger-word banks, subject
length, ALL CAPS, punctuation runs, emoji, faked `Re:` prefixes, currency and
percentage symbols, phone numbers, link count and link domains, unfilled merge
tokens, and body length. It also checks the one thing a single-email checker
cannot see: **how many rows send an identical subject or body**, which is a
stronger spam signal than any word inside them.

Result: **zero HIGH findings, zero MEDIUM.** Every body on every row is
distinct. The only remaining note is 33 message-3 bodies at 39 words, which is
the deliberate two-line version for thread B.

Two findings were real and are fixed:

- **Seven prospects write their own tagline in capitals**, and quoting them
  verbatim put `EXPLORE CERAMIC + PORCELAIN` and `WE MANUFACTURE TO MEASURE,
  ACCORDING TO YOU WISHES` inside the body. Capitalised words score on every
  public checker. A tagline shouted end to end now folds to sentence case; a
  shouted word inside normal text, `PORCELANOSA`, `VIVES`, title-cases and
  keeps its capital. **The words never change** — they are the prospect's own,
  inside quotation marks, typos included.
- **Eight company names carried a shouted token**, so bodies read `Cotto
  D'ESTE` and `Aixin TECH.CO`. Same fold, applied after the existing
  title-casing rather than before it.

Two hits are verified as ordinary language and reported under their own heading
so nobody spends time removing them: **"sale"** appears inside the company name
Fabbrica del Sale and inside two prospects' own quoted site copy, *"manufacture
and sale of ceramic wall and floor tiles"*; **"sample"** is the entire argument
of the cost-side email.

`templates_for_spamcheck.md` holds all **24 distinct template shapes** in the
file with personalisation filled in from a real row, so the same set can go
through Mail Meteor or GlockApps by hand for a second opinion in a couple of
minutes.

## Bugs caught building v5

- **Sentence-casing was applied to every company name, not just the shouted
  ones.** Caught before it shipped. 361 of 439 names arrive from the
  directories in capitals, and folding them before the title-casing rule ran
  left bodies reading "Atlas concorde" and "Akgun seramik". The two folds are
  now separate functions and the brand one runs after the title-casing, not
  before.
- **A name opening on a connector kept shouting.** "LA FABBRICA AVA" came out
  as "LA Fabbrica AVA", because the acronym rule keeps anything of three
  letters or fewer as written and "LA" is three letters. A leading connector
  is a word, not an acronym.
- **Three names carried a directory descriptor after the brand**, so a body
  read "Jadrankamen white limestone Croatia/EU". A lower-case word following a
  capitalised one is the signal, since brands do not do that except through a
  connector. Checked against all 439 names: it fires on those three and
  nothing else.
- **`subject1` had become a one-line wrapper with 60 lines of unreachable code
  behind an early `return`**, and the `S1` bank it used was dead with it. The
  bank still held the line the review quoted, `Traffic {dom} can't put a name
  to`, which is why that line reads as current when it has not been sent to a
  file since 24 Sept. Both removed, and the six call sites now call
  `show_subject` directly.
