## SECTION 1 — THE FRAMEWORKS, AND WHICH TWO WE USE

### The common cold-email frameworks

| # | Framework | Structure | Best for | Why it fits / fails MEC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AIDA** | Attention → Interest → Desire → Action | Ads, landing pages | ❌ **Reject.** The "Desire" step needs a claim that makes them *want* it. MEC has **no hard numbers** (§0). Building desire without proof means inventing it — breaks the honest-proof rule |
| 2 | **PAS** | Problem → Agitate → Solution | Pain-led outbound | ⚠️ **Risky.** "Agitate" means telling them their pain is worse than they think. We got this wrong on Jubi — assumed a manual loop, they had a configurator. Agitating an assumed pain to a stranger is how you look uninformed |
| 3 | **BAB** | Before → After → Bridge | When the "after" is demonstrable | ✅ **Use as Variant B.** MEC's "after" is a live clickable link — the one thing we can show rather than claim |
| 4 | **House 4-block** (standing rule 13) | Observation → Consequence → Proof → Low-commitment CTA | Triminage standard | ✅ **Use as Variant A.** This *is* the Campaign 2 fix. It must be tested or the fix stays unproven |
| 5 | **QVC** | Question → Value → Close | Short nurture | ➖ Opening a cold email with a question invites "no" as the easiest reply |
| 6 | **SPIN** | Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff | Discovery **calls** | ➖ Needs dialogue. Wrong medium |
| 7 | **Two-sentence / sniper** | Observation + ask | Very senior buyers | ➖ Hold in reserve for the 200+ headcount rows (§7.4 tags these long-cycle anyway) |

### ✅ The two we are testing

> **VARIANT A — HOUSE 4-BLOCK** *(primary)*
> `Verifiable observation → Commercial consequence → Proof → Low-commitment CTA`
> This is standing rule 13. Campaign 2 failed on capability-framed copy; this structure is the designed fix and it is still **unproven**. It has to be in the test.

> **VARIANT B — BAB** *(challenger)*
> `Before (their today) → After (with a renderer) → Bridge (the live link)`
> Chosen because MEC's differentiator is that the proof *is* a working tool. BAB is the only framework whose middle step is naturally satisfied by "here, open it."

## SECTION 2 — WHAT WE MAY AND MAY NOT SAY

Straight from §0's honest-proof rule. This is the hard boundary on every line below.

| ✅ Safe to claim | ❌ Never claim |
|---|---|
| Built in **7 weeks** with a team of **3** (re-verified 24 Aug) | Any percentage, any hours-saved figure |
| **Live** at ai.mecartworks.com | Any revenue or conversion number |
| "In **active use** by the MEC Artworks team" | Any named MEC customer |
| Renders in **real time** / **seconds** (qualitative only) | CureMD's 98% / 15-second figures — wrong client, wrong campaign |
| Next.js, AI integration, canvas rendering | That we know *their* internal process — we know their **website** |

**The Jubi rule (new, learned the hard way):** never assert that a prospect does something *by hand* or *lacks* a tool. We were wrong once on the best-researched name in the file. Every observation below is phrased from what their **site or their job titles** verifiably say — never from what we assume happens inside.

## SECTION 7 — HOW TO READ THE RESULTS

### Measure these only

| Metric | Use |
|---|---|
| **Reply rate** | Primary |
| **Positive-reply rate** | The real one |
| **Link clicks** (touch 2+) | Self-qualification signal — §7.9 says anyone who plays has self-identified |
| ~~Open rate~~ | **Ignore.** Learning #2 — machine opens make it noise |

### ⚠️ An honest warning about the A/B

You have **42 sendable rows**. Split two ways, that is **21 per arm**. At a realistic 5–10% reply rate, each arm returns **1–2 replies**.

**Two replies versus one is not a result.** It is noise. No split of this list can tell you which framework is better with any confidence.

**So I would not run it as a statistical test.** Recommended instead:

1. **Run Variant A (house 4-block) on all 42.** It is the standing rule and the unproven Campaign 2 fix — the thing you actually need an answer on.
2. **Read the replies qualitatively.** With numbers this small, *what people say* carries far more information than the rate. "Wrong person" ≠ "not interested" ≠ "we already have one."
3. **Hold Variant B in reserve.** If A returns near-zero after ~20 sends, switch rather than split — a sequential switch beats an underpowered split.
4. **If you insist on one split, split the highest-leverage variable** — the §11 link-in-touch-1 vs touch-2 test. That changes behaviour far more than sentence structure does.

This is the same trap as Campaign 2's open rates: a number that looks like evidence and isn't.
